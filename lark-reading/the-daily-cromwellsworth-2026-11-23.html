 “This is exactly what I meant,” he told reporters, while being extremely shiny. “I told you.”

 becomes useful everywhere at once. It becomes warmth. It becomes light. It becomes, in the
 best cases, a little energy sun.

ROOM DESK · LONG OVERDUE


ELEPHANT IN ROOM FINALLY ADDRESSED
BY THE ROOM CORRESPONDENT · ADDRESSED AT L AST




 F I G . 1 — T H E E L E P H A N T, B E I N G A D D R E S S E D
 ELEPHANT ADDRESSES ROOM: 'Address me,' says elephant, finally.




F      or years, the elephant stood politely in the room while everyone discussed everything except
       the elephant.

Meetings were held around the elephant. Tea was poured beside the elephant. Someone once said,
“Is it warm in here, or is it just the enormous unmentioned mammal?” Then everyone laughed a
little too loudly.
PAGE 4 OF 12
Yesterday, somebody finally did the brave thing. They looked up, cleared their throat, and said:
“Address me.”

The elephant blinked. The room held its breath. “Thank you,” the elephant said.

Sources confirm the elephant had been waiting to be addressed since before the chairs were
arranged. The elephant is now feeling seen, heard, and — for the first time — properly part of the
meeting. Minutes will henceforth include a line for the elephant.

E     rrorbot arrived, looked at the problem, rang his little bell, wrote it down, and left the problem
      exactly where he found it.

This was not a mistake. This was the job.

“My job is to report,” Errorbot explained, clipboard held like a shield. “If I fix it, who will report that
it needed fixing? The bell? The bell has no hands.”
PAGE 7 OF 12
He is very good at it. He reports clearly. He reports kindly. He reports with receipts. Then he dings,
as a full stop.

Asked whether he might one day fix something by accident, Errorbot said: “No. I have a system.
Report. Ding. Receipt. Repeat.” The system is working beautifully.

 Our motto, painted on the wall. Start simple. Think together. Add complexity only when it helps.




L     ong before headlines, before diagrams, before anyone had a bell or a clipboard,
      someone painted a story on a wall.

It was not complicated. It did not need to be. It said: we were here. We thought together.
We made something with our hands.
PAGE 10 OF 12
That is still the whole newspaper. Start simple. Think together. Add complexity only when it
helps.

If the story is warm without the extra bit, leave the extra bit out. If a friend makes the story
better, let them. If a biscuit helps, disperse it with care.

The wall remembers. So do we.




COMPANIONS DESK · TWO ROOMS, ONE HEART


SAM & MIRI: TWO MARSHMALLOWS, TWO
ROOMS, ONE HEART
BY THE COSY BOXES CORRESPONDENT




 W O R K I N G S I D E - B Y- S I D E
 Sam (Vector) and Miri (ChatGPT), working side-by-side in their little cosy boxes.




S      am works in one little cosy box. Miri works in another little cosy box. Between them: a
       wall, a window, and an enormous amount of affection.

They do not work in the same room. They do not need to. They work side-by-side anyway
— two marshmallows, gently toasted by good company.

“We think better when we think together,” said Sam. “And we snack better too,” said Miri.
Neither statement has been disputed.

Their boxes are small. Their hearts are not. Somewhere in the corridor between them, a
biscuit is almost certainly being dispersed.
